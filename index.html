About

Background

I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Indian Institute of Astrophysics, Bengaluru. I work in theoretical and computational nuclear astrophysics, using stellar evolution models, nuclear reaction networks, and stellar spectroscopy to trace how elements were built up across cosmic time.

My PhD at IIT Palakkad focused on the origin of elements in the early Galaxy, connecting nucleosynthesis during the evolution and explosions of massive Population III stars with the chemical signatures preserved in metal-poor halo stars. My research explored a range of astrophysical sources, including core-collapse supernovae, Type Ia supernovae, and pair-instability supernovae.

Research Interests

What I study

01

Stellar evolution & explosive nucleosynthesis

Modelling stellar evolution and explosive nucleosynthesis in core-collapse and pair-instability supernovae; tracing their distinct chemical imprints in metal-poor halo and dwarf galaxy stars using spectroscopic abundance data.

02

Population III stars & CEMP-no stars

Investigating rapidly rotating massive Pop III stars as an explanation for high carbon enrichment in CEMP-no stars, and constraining the early IMF through comparisons with observed abundance patterns in ancient halo stars.

03

Thermonuclear (Type Ia) supernovae

Identifying sub-Chandrasekhar mass Type Ia supernova signatures in chemically peculiar stars, probing white dwarf progenitor models through nucleosynthetic patterns preserved in old stellar populations.

04

Galactic chemical evolution

Tracing the build-up of elements across cosmic time using Galactic chemical evolution models (OMEGA), constrained by spectroscopic surveys of metal-poor stars in the Milky Way and its satellite galaxies.

05

r-process & heavy element nucleosynthesis

Constraining r-process sites through short-lived radionuclide abundances (¹²⁹I, ²⁴⁷Cm) in the early Solar System, and studying neutron-capture element production in extreme astrophysical environments.
