About

Background

I am a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Indian Institute of Astrophysics, Bengaluru. I work in theoretical and computational nuclear astrophysics, using stellar evolution models, nuclear reaction networks, and stellar spectroscopy to trace how elements were built up across cosmic time.

My PhD at IIT Palakkad focused on the origin of elements in the early Galaxy, connecting nucleosynthesis during the evolution and explosions of massive Population III stars with the chemical signatures preserved in metal-poor halo stars. My research explored a range of astrophysical sources, including core-collapse supernovae, Type Ia supernovae, and pair-instability supernovae.

01

Stellar evolution & explosive nucleosynthesis

Developing systematic grids of core-collapse supernova (CCSN) and pair-instability supernova (PISN) models for Population III stars using KEPLER, with detailed nucleosynthetic yield calculations. A key finding is that rapid rotation fundamentally alters PISN chemical fingerprints. This makes PISN signatures harder to identify in metal-poor stars and can have a physically motivated explanation for the anomalous nitrogen enrichment observed by JWST in high-redshift galaxies.

02

Population III stars & CEMP-no stars

Investigating rapidly rotating massive Pop III stars that reach a quasi-chemically homogeneous (QCH) state as an explanation for the high carbon enrichment observed in CEMP-no stars. QCH stars eject carbon-rich winds that reproduce almost the entire observed range of carbon abundances in CEMP-no stars, providing the first systematic theoretical support for rapid rotation as a key ingredient in the earliest stellar populations.

03

Thermonuclear (Type Ia) supernovae in the early Galaxy

Identifying the nucleosynthetic signatures of sub-Chandrasekhar mass Type Ia supernovae in chemically peculiar very metal-poor stars, which reveals that thermonuclear explosions from helium-detonating white dwarfs contributed to the chemical enrichment of the Galaxy within its first billion years. Extending this work to dwarf galaxies, including the Sculptor dwarf and the Large Magellanic Cloud, to establish a coherent picture of early thermonuclear enrichment across different environments.

04

Stellar spectroscopy & abundance analysis

Measuring detailed elemental abundances from high-resolution optical spectra of metal-poor stars using synthesis codes including TURBOSPECTRUM and IRAF, with non-LTE corrections applied wherever model atoms are available. This observational work spans halo field stars, CEMP and NEMP stars, and stars in dwarf galaxies.

05

Pulsations & dust formation in red supergiants

Modelling pulsation-driven mass loss in red supergiants such as VY Canis Majoris using MESA and KEPLER with time-dependent convection schemes. Radial pulsations grow to supersonic amplitudes in the extended stellar envelopes, triggering shock breakout and episodic dynamical ejections that dominate the mass budget over steady winds. This work connects to dust formation in evolved massive stars and supernova environments.

06

Galactic chemical evolution

Tracing the enrichments of elements across cosmic time using Galactic chemical evolution models (OMEGA), constrained by spectroscopic surveys of metal-poor stars in the Milky Way halo and Local Group dwarf spheroidal galaxies. Modelling the chemical enrichment histories of the Milky Way halo, thick disc, and satellite galaxies using data from large surveys including GALAH, APOGEE, and 4MOST.

07

r-process & heavy element nucleosynthesis

Constraining r-process nucleosynthesis sites through short-lived radionuclide abundances (₉I and ′‴₇Cm) in the early Solar System, and studying neutron-capture element production in extreme astrophysical environments. Connecting r-process yields to the heavy element abundance patterns observed in metal-poor halo and dwarf galaxy stars.
